Marathon fueling

Target approximately 60 g carbohydrate/hour, potentially more if well tolerated. For a ~4:30 marathon, that's roughly 270 g carbohydrate during the race.

Use a combination of
  • Precision Fuel
  • Honey Stinger
  • Aid-station fluids
Don't rely entirely on gels.
Practice during long runs
  • How much Precision you can drink per hour
  • Honey Stinger timing
  • Water needs
  • Carrying a bottle if desired
  • Fueling after 2–3 hours

Race-morning nutrition

Approximately 2.5–3 hours before the start:

  • Rice cakes
  • Banana
  • Honey/jam
  • Water
  • Precision and/or Liquid I.V. if desired and already tolerated

Optional: Honey Stinger 10–15 min before the start.

Do not introduce new foods or supplements on race morning.

Race strategy by mile

MilesPlan
1–510:30–10:40/mi. Should feel very easy. Don't get pulled into a faster pace by the excitement of the start.
6–15Gradually move toward 10:15–10:25/mi. If feeling fantastic, stay controlled — don't speed up significantly.
16–20Assess your legs. Feeling strong: ~10:15–10:25/mi. Struggling: slow toward 10:30–11:00/mi, fuel, hydrate, stay calm.
21–26.2Decide whether to race. Energy left: gradually increase effort. Struggling: slow down, walk aid stations if necessary, fuel, drink, resume running.

Week 41–2 very light sessions, mostly upper body
Race weekNo lifting
Avoid: heavy squats to failure, heavy deadlifts, high-volume leg workouts, hard leg training within ~48 hours of long runs. The priority is running durability and recovery.

Decision point: the 17-mile long run

This run (Sunday, Nov 22) is the most important indicator for your marathon strategy.

If you...
  • Complete 16–18 miles
  • Fuel successfully
  • Maintain controlled effort
  • Finish tired but functional
  • Recover normally over the next few days
Then 4:30 becomes a legitimate stretch target.
If you...
  • Struggle badly before 14–15 miles
  • Develop significant pain
  • Cannot fuel adequately
  • Need extensive walking
  • Take several days to recover
Then switch race strategy to finish-first rather than chasing 4:30.

Priorities

  • Stay healthy
  • Complete the 16–18 mile peak long run
  • Practice marathon fueling
  • Keep easy runs genuinely easy
  • Reduce lifting enough to recover
  • Taper
  • Race intelligently

Your 1:51:23 half marathon shows that your aerobic fitness is strong enough to make 4:30 plausible. The unknown is marathon-specific durability, especially after mile 18. This plan is designed to test and improve that durability without trying to cram an entire traditional marathon buildup into five weeks.
